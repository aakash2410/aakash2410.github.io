2.1

Inverse-Objective Regulator

Research system · Sole author · Source ↗

A black-box auditor that works out what a deployed AI agent is actually optimising for, using only its tool-call traces. Bayesian inverse reinforcement learning recovers the agent's revealed objective and scores how far it drifts from its declared purpose. The output is evidence a regulator can read, mapped to the EU AI Act, OWASP, MITRE ATLAS, and NIST AI RMF and emitted as JSON and SARIF.

It ships with an open 8-agent benchmark of agentic failure modes: reward hacking, power-seeking, sycophancy, privacy leakage, and safety-skipping.

Sole-authored arXiv system paper. The benchmark is public, so others can test their auditors against it.

Bayesian IRL, LLM and decision-model judges, and a reproducible gym. The open critic has near-zero variance where the LLM judge doesn't.

Turns an abstract safety risk into evidence a regulator can use, in formats that existing security tooling already reads.

2.3

Credit Equity Audit of India

Independent fairness audit · NSSO 77th Round · Source ↗

Who gets credit in India, and on what terms? A two-stage, Heckman-style deep learning model separates access to the credit market from the allocation of formal versus informal credit among households already in it. IBM AIF360 adversarial debiasing and SHAP then test for proxy discrimination, reaching near-ideal disparate impact at ~73.2% accuracy.

The audit states its own limits: self-reported survey data, the risk of collider bias, and SHAP as a diagnostic of the model rather than causal evidence.

Treats access as a selection problem before asking about allocation. Being honest about limits is part of the result.

Stage 1 predicts market participation, stage 2 predicts formal vs. informal allocation.

A 0.59 is the kind of number a lender or regulator has to act on.
